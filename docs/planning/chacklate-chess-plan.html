01

Executive summary & product definition

What we are building, and the shape of the recommendation

Confirmed constraints (treated as fixed)

  • Premium full-3D visuals with reduced-effects and low-graphics options.
  • Native Swift/SwiftUI on iOS and native Kotlin on Android, with a shared backend, chess core, content definitions and domain logic.
  • On-device AI first: rules, engine analysis and core coaching work fully offline. No cloud AI in core gameplay or coaching.
  • Free tier with ads, Premium subscription, lifetime purchase and optional cosmetics. Chapters 1–3 free, the rest included in Premium. No per-chapter sales by default.
  • Simultaneous public launch on iOS, Android, PC/Mac and web, with online multiplayer available at launch.
  • Three deliverables with separate identities: (A) the game, (B) the marketing website, (C) the admin/operations application.

Recommendation in one paragraph

Build one portable chess core in Rust (rules, PGN/FEN, clocks, result logic) that is compiled into every client and into the game server, so a rated result is computed by the same code everywhere. Put native SwiftUI and Jetpack Compose shells around a shared 3D scene runtime, and serve PC/Mac by packaging the web client in a lightweight native shell. The rendering technology is the single most consequential choice; the plan runs a short bake-off between a Filament-based shared scene runtime (lead candidate) and Unity 6 as a Library (challenger) before committing. Coaching is engine-first: every claim comes from the engine and a deterministic tactic detector, and an on-device language model, where the device supports one, only rephrases those verified facts.

Candid feasibility view. A simultaneous four-platform 3D launch with multiplayer, 74 narrated lessons and seven cosmetic categories is achievable, but realistically needs about 20–24 months and a peak team of roughly 38–45 people. Keeping the launch date and all four platforms is credible if launch catalogue counts are set below the 20-per-category targets (those become the 12-month targets) and the lower-priority distinctive features move to post-launch. Section 16 shows the alternatives if the budget or timeline is tighter.

Product pillars

Play beautifully

Premium 3D boards, pieces and rooms, with an always-available clean top-down view for readability and fast time controls.

Learn measurably

74 voice-guided lessons with mastery checks, and progress measured by assessments, tactic accuracy and blunder trends, never promised rating gains.

Understand every move

An offline coach that explains threats, plans and mistakes using verified engine lines, with adjustable hints and verbosity.

Compete fairly

Server-authoritative rated play, separate rating pools, seasons and leaderboards, and a published fair-play policy.

Home screen (kept deliberately small)

Four primary actions: Quick Play, Continue Learning, Play a Friend, Improve My Chess. Everything else (puzzles, analysis board, tournaments, seasons, collection, store, settings) lives in a secondary tab bar or menu.

Time controls (rules, separate from clock skins)

Bullet (<3 min), Blitz (3–9 min), Rapid (10–29 min), Classical (30+ min), plus custom. Increment (Fischer) and delay (Bronstein and simple delay) are supported in the core. 05

Visual direction & 3D art bible

Proposed direction for approval, plus the technical rules every asset follows

Proposed direction: "Tempered"

The name Chacklate suggests the craft of a chocolatier: tempered gloss, cocoa woods, cream stone, gold foil and ruby accents. The proposal is to borrow those materials and the atelier mood while keeping the game unmistakably chess: classical Staunton-derived silhouettes for the default set, calm rooms, and a signature ruby-chocolate accent used sparingly for selection and highlights. It must never look like a confectionery or casual game. The brand identity (logo, wordmark, app icon) is a separate commission and depends on trademark clearance (section 18).

Readability rules (non-negotiable for every board and piece set)

  • Light and dark squares keep a minimum luminance contrast that is checked automatically in the asset pipeline; pieces must remain distinguishable from both square colours under every lighting preset.
  • King, queen, rook, bishop, knight and pawn must be identified correctly in a 32 px top-down silhouette test and a 3/4 camera test, by at least 95% of testers (including colour-blind testers).
  • Move, check, last-move and premove highlights use shape plus colour, never colour alone; colour-blind palettes ship at launch.
  • A clean top-down "Tournament view" is always one tap away, renders with no room, and is the default for bullet.
  • Cosmetics never change hitboxes, legal targets, animation timing in timed games, or visibility. Capture and victory effects are skipped automatically when the player's clock is under 10 seconds in online play.

Catalogue item definition (data-driven, no item hardcoded in screens)

Each item has: stable ID (board.walnut_gold.v1 style, never reused), category, display name keys, rarity, collection, price list by product SKU, unlock conditions (purchase, Premium, season reward, achievement, free), compatibility (e.g. furniture valid for which rooms), preview model reference, asset bundle versions per platform and tier, minimum client version, availability window, and a fallback item ID. The client downloads a signed catalogue manifest; new items and price changes ship without an app update, while new shader or code features are gated by minimum client version.

Production pipeline

  1. Concept and silhouette review (art director plus chess readability check).
  2. Modelling to budget, LODs, materials from the shared library.
  3. Automated validation: triangle and texture budgets, naming, square-contrast check, glTF validator.
  4. Build per platform and tier, upload to the asset CDN with content hashes.
  5. Preview on device matrix, then publish through the admin console with approval.

Outsourcing to a 3D art vendor is assumed for about half of launch assets, with in-house art direction and technical art owning quality.

Narration

Roughly 6 minutes of narration per lesson gives about 7.5 hours of audio. At 48 kbps Opus that is about 160 MB per language, downloaded per chapter. Recommended default: professional human voice for English at launch (best perceived quality, clear rights), with synthetic voice only from a provider whose licence covers commercial redistribution, if used for later languages. Players get pause, replay segment, 0.75×–1.5× speed, captions and full transcripts. No lesson requires speaking.

Measuring improvement honestly

Track per-skill assessment accuracy, tactic puzzle accuracy by motif, blunders per 40 moves in real games, threat-recognition speed, and retention on spaced reviews. The product reports these trends; it does not promise a rating increase unless a properly designed study later supports a claim.

08

On-device AI & chess-engine specification

Engine-first coaching, licensing, and a device capability matrix

Coaching architecture

  1. Chess core validates positions and moves.
  2. Engine produces evaluations, principal variations and multi-PV candidates.
  3. Deterministic analysers classify each move (best, good, inaccuracy, mistake, blunder, missed win) by evaluation and win-probability loss, and detect motifs: forks, pins, skewers, discovered attacks, hanging pieces, back-rank weakness, stalemate traps, king exposure.
  4. Fact sheet: a structured, verified record of all the above for the position.
  5. Explainer: templated, chess-reviewed sentences generate the explanation on every device. Where an on-device language model is available, it may rephrase the fact sheet or answer a question about it, but its output is checked: any move, square or evaluation it mentions must appear in the fact sheet, otherwise the templated text is shown.

The language model never chooses or calculates moves. If the fact sheet cannot answer a question, the coach says so and offers the deterministic explanation.

What "a 3-move plan" means

Defined in the player's own moves, each followed by the opponent's reply, starting and ending with the player's move. An N-move plan is 2N − 1 plies. The UI shows "3 moves (5 plies)" in the help text.

Engine licensing (decision required)

Stockfish, Leela Chess Zero and most top open-source engines are licensed under GPL-3.0. Embedding one inside a closed-source iOS or Android app would require releasing the app's corresponding source under GPL, and App Store distribution terms are widely considered to conflict with GPL. Stockfish's own neural networks carry the same licence. This needs a legal opinion, but the plan should not depend on it.

Screenshot and import pipeline

Board detection (corner finding plus perspective correction), a small square classifier (13 classes) trained on synthetic renders of our own piece sets plus licensed or self-captured screenshots, orientation detection from coordinates and piece placement, candidate FEN, legality check by the chess core, then an edit screen where the player confirms or fixes uncertain squares. Runs with Core ML, TensorFlow Lite / LiteRT and ONNX Runtime Web. Images are processed locally and discarded after confirmation unless the player saves the position.

Fair-play boundary

The coach, hints, evaluation bar and analysis are disabled in all online games while the game is live, including casual. They unlock the moment the game ends. The app cannot stop someone using another device, so fair-play detection still happens on the server (section 12).

Cost principle

Offline engine and coaching have no marginal service cost, so there are no AI credits. Premium gates advanced features (deep review, training plans, opening lab), not compute.

Infrastructure (default)

API principles

  • Versioned contracts (/v1, protobuf packages chacklate.game.v1) with backward-compatible evolution and a minimum-client-version gate.
  • All writes that grant value (purchases, rating changes, rewards, inventory) require an idempotency key and are recorded in append-only ledgers.
  • Public data (profile card, leaderboard rows) served from separate read models from private data (email, purchases, precise settings).
  • Rate limits per account, device and IP; signed request timestamps to reject replays.

Multiplayer, rating, tournament & fair-play specification

Server-authoritative from the first rated game

Realtime protocol

  • WebSocket over TLS, protobuf messages, one connection per client multiplexing games.
  • Each game is a single-writer actor on one node, holding authoritative position, clocks and sequence number. Events are persisted before acknowledgement.
  • Client sends MoveIntent{game_id, expected_ply, uci, client_move_id}. The server rejects wrong ply, illegal move or duplicate id, and broadcasts MoveApplied{seq, uci, clocks, server_ts}.
  • Clocks run on server time. Lag compensation credits measured network delay up to a cap (for example 1 s per move in blitz) to keep fast games fair without letting a client claim unlimited lag.
  • Reconnect: client presents a reconnect token and last seq; server replays missing events. Clients show "Opponent reconnecting" with a countdown.

Game-ending rules

SituationRule (default)
No first moveGame aborted, no rating change, if a player doesn't move within 30 s (bullet/blitz) or 60 s (rapid+)
DisconnectClock keeps running; after 30 s (bullet/blitz) or 60 s (rapid+) of absence the opponent may claim a win or a draw
TimeoutLoss, unless the opponent has insufficient mating material, then draw (decided by the core)
Draw offersOne pending offer at a time, limited per game to prevent spam; threefold and 50-move claimable, fivefold and 75-move automatic
Repeated abandonmentEscalating matchmaking cooldowns, not instant bans

Matchmaking

Pools per time control and rated/casual. The search starts at ±100 rating and widens over time, preferring players in the same region. At launch, liquidity is a real risk: if no match is found within 45 s, offer a rated-pool widening or a casual game against an AI clearly labelled as a computer. Bots are never presented as humans.

Ratings

Glicko-2, separate pools for bullet, blitz, rapid, classical, puzzles, and each future variant. New players start at 1500 with high rating deviation and a provisional marker for their first 20 games. Shown as "Chacklate rating", never compared to FIDE. Rating updates happen only on the server, idempotently per game id.

Leaderboards and seasons

  • Global, country, city, friends and seasonal boards per pool, plus puzzle and tournament boards. Minimum 20 rated games and recent activity to appear.
  • Country from store region with user override; city is opt-in, chosen from a list, never derived from GPS. Players can hide their location entirely.
  • Seasons default to 8 weeks (6–8 configurable). Ratings never reset; seasons track season points from games, missions and puzzles. Lifetime history, peak ratings, achievements and past season ranks are permanent.
  • Season config is server-side and versioned; every reward grant has an idempotency key and audit record.

Tournaments (launch-lite)

Arena and Swiss formats for scheduled public events and private friend/club tournaments, using the same game service. Pairing and standings are computed on the server.

Fair play

  • Policy published in plain language: no outside assistance in live games; analysis allowed after the game.
  • Signals: engine-match rates and centipawn loss relative to rating, move-time patterns, focus-loss events reported by the client (weak signal), rating jumps, account links.
  • Signals open a case for human review; no automatic severe penalty from statistics alone. Ladder: warning, rated-play restriction, rating removal, suspension, ban. Every action is appealable and logged.
  • Ratings of affected opponents are restored when a cheater is confirmed.

Security, privacy, accessibility, testing & release

Targets are provisional until PoCs and the device matrix confirm them

Security

  • OAuth 2.0 / OIDC sign-in, short-lived access tokens with rotating refresh tokens, device-bound sessions, Sign in with Apple wherever other social logins are offered on iOS.
  • Server-authoritative competitive state, receipt validation server-to-server, idempotency keys, replay protection, rate limits, bot detection on signup.
  • Dependency and container scanning in CI, signed builds, SBOM per release, secrets in a managed vault, annual penetration test before launch and yearly after.
  • Encrypted backups with tested restores (quarterly drill), defined RPO 15 min and RTO 4 h for the platform database.
  • Vulnerability disclosure policy and incident response runbooks.

Privacy and child safety

  • India's DPDP Act defines children as under 18 and requires verifiable parental consent and no behavioural tracking or targeted ads for them. Built (October 2026, game directed to children): neutral age gate, parental consent by email for every under-18 account, no ads of any kind, no chat, kids-only matchmaking, parent controls and PIN-approved purchases. COPPA (US, under 13) and GDPR rules for EU minors handled the same way. The owner reviews the policy and terms with a mapped self-review checklist (no outside lawyer); see docs/planning/children.md.
  • Consent management where required (EU/UK), App Tracking Transparency on iOS, Google Play Data Safety and Apple privacy labels kept accurate.
  • Coach questions, screenshots and documents stay on the device. Analytics never includes chess conversation text or imported files.

Accessibility

VoiceOver and TalkBack support with spoken board state and move announcements; keyboard play on web/desktop (type e4 or Nf3); dynamic type; colour-blind board and highlight palettes; high-contrast boards; Reduced Effects; captions and transcripts for all narration; no time pressure in lessons. Web, website and admin target WCAG 2.2 AA.
