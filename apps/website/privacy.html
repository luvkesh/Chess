Privacy for families

Last updated 30 October 2026 · Beta. Details shown in [brackets] are filled in before public launch.

For kids

Chackmate is a game for learning and playing chess. Here is how we look after you:

  • You can play the computer, do lessons and solve puzzles without telling us anything about you.
  • Before you can have your own account with a saved name, ratings and progress, a grown-up who looks after you has to say yes.
  • We give you a made-up player name, like BraveOtter42, so nobody knows who you are.
  • There is no chat, so nobody can send you messages.
  • We never show you ads, and we never sell anything about you.
  • If you have a question, or something feels wrong, tell your grown-up. They can see your account, change it or delete it at any time.
For parents, in short. The game works fully offline and collects nothing in that mode. Children can try online games as anonymous guests; a child's own account needs a parent's yes first. We keep only what the game needs: a made-up player name, a password hash, birth month and year, games and progress. No ads, no third-party analytics or tracking, no chat, no selling or sharing for anyone's marketing. From the parent page you can see, change, download or delete everything, at any time.

1. Who we are

Chackmate is run by Chacklate Private Limited, [registered address], Mumbai, India ("we", "us"). We decide how personal data in the game is used, so we are responsible for it (the "data fiduciary" under India's DPDP Act and the "controller" under the GDPR).

Privacy questions and requests: legal@chacklate.com. Grievance officer (India): Luvkesh Agarwal, legal@chacklate.com.

2. What this policy covers

This policy covers the Chackmate game on the web and in our apps, this website, and the parent page. It covers players of every age, and parents and guardians. It does not cover app stores or payment pages run by others, which have their own policies. The game itself contains no links to other websites; any way out of the game sits behind a check for grown-ups.

3. Playing without an account

Games against the computer, lessons, puzzles and the endgame dojo run on the device. Progress is saved in the device's own storage. Nothing about the player is sent to us.

Anyone can also try online games as a guest. A guest gets a random name such as Guest4821 and a random code saved on the device, so a dropped game can reconnect. We do not ask a guest for any personal information. Guests are matched only with other young players and guests.

4. Ages and a parent's permission

When someone makes an account, we ask for their birth month and year (never the day), without hinting at a "right" answer. That decides which rules apply:

  • Under 18: we treat the player as a child, as India's DPDP Act does (the US COPPA rule covers under 13, and EU and UK rules cover under 13 to 16 depending on the country; we apply the strictest rules to everyone under 18). The player picks a name we make up and gives a parent's email address.
  • 18 and over: a normal account. The player chooses a name, which is checked for rude words and contact details.

How a parent gives permission. We send one email to the address the child gives, with a link that works for 7 days. The parent reads what we keep, chooses what the child may do, confirms they are the parent or legal guardian, and creates a parent account with their own email, password and a purchase PIN. Until then the child's account stays offline-only: no online play, no progress saved to our server, no reminders and no purchases. We do not store the parent's email unless they say yes; if nobody answers, the request expires and the address is not used again.

Parents can withdraw permission at any time from the parent page. We then delete the child's account (see section 11).

6. What we never do

  • Show ads of any kind, or use data for advertising or profiling.
  • Use third-party analytics, advertising tools, social logins or tracking pixels.
  • Sell, rent or share personal data for anyone's marketing.
  • Offer chat. Players cannot send each other messages.
  • Show public profiles of under-18s or list them on public leaderboards.
  • Sell random rewards (loot boxes), use countdown pressure, or send messages meant to make a child feel guilty for not playing.
  • Make decisions about a child by automated means that have legal or similarly important effects. Fair-play flags are always reviewed by a person before any action.

7. What stays on the device

The game saves settings, offline progress, the day's play time (for the daily limit a parent can set) and a sign-in code in the browser's or app's own storage, and keeps game files (including voice recordings and pictures of Collection items) so it works offline and loads quickly. These are needed for the game to work; none are used to track anyone. We do not use advertising cookies. Clearing the browser's site data, or deleting the app, removes them.

8. Notifications

Notifications are off unless turned on. For under-18s, a parent must allow them on the parent page first. There is one kind: a calm daily puzzle reminder at a time the player picks, never with deadlines or streak warnings. The reminder is delivered by the push service built into the browser or phone (run by Google, Apple or Mozilla, depending on the device). That service sees only a device address and an encrypted message it cannot read, never who the player is. Turning reminders off deletes the push address.

Only with service providers who help us run the game, under contracts that let them use the data only for that work and require them to protect it:

  • Hosting for the game server and backups: [hosting provider].
  • Sending the parent permission email and account emails: [email provider].

10. Where data is kept

Our servers and backups are in India. If data is handled in another country, we use the safeguards the law requires there (for example, the European Commission's standard contractual clauses) and keep the same protections.

12. Your rights and how to use them

Parents can, at any time and for free:

  • see what the child does and change what they may do, on the parent page;
  • download all the data we hold about the child, from the parent page;
  • correct details, or ask us to stop collecting anything more;
  • delete the child's account, or withdraw permission and delete everything, from the parent page;
  • name someone to act for the child if the parent cannot (India's DPDP Act).

Adult players can download or delete their data in Settings, and correct their details.

You can also write to legal@chacklate.com. To protect children, we check that a request comes from the account's parent (usually by asking you to sign in to the parent page or reply from the parent email). We answer within 30 days, and usually much sooner.

Kids: you can ask your grown-up to look at your account with you, fix anything wrong, or delete it.

13. Safety, reports and moderation

Players under 18 are matched only with other young players and guests. A parent of a teenager can allow games with adults. Friends play together by sharing a short code that works only between players in the same group. Anyone can report a game; a person reviews every report. Where the law requires, we report child-safety concerns to the authorities.

14. Purchases

The store sits behind a check for grown-ups. On an under-18 account every purchase needs the parent's PIN, which locks after repeated wrong guesses, and a parent can turn purchases off. Everything for sale is described exactly; nothing is random.

15. Security and breaches

Passwords and PINs are stored only as salted hashes. Connections are encrypted. Staff tools are limited to named people with the least access they need, and every staff action is written to a log that cannot be quietly changed. No system is perfectly secure: if a breach affects personal data, we tell affected parents and players without delay, and we inform the Data Protection Board of India within 72 hours and other authorities as their laws require.

16. Extra notes by country

India

We follow the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and its Rules: verifiable parental consent for everyone under 18, no tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted ads aimed at children, and a grievance officer (section 1). If you are not satisfied with our answer, you can complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

United States

We follow the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA). We do not sell or share personal data, as those words are used in California law, and we do not use it for targeted advertising.

European Union and United Kingdom

We follow the GDPR and the UK Age Appropriate Design Code: high privacy by default, the legal bases in section 5, and parental consent for children under the age set by each country. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

17. Changes to this policy

We show the date of the latest version at the top. If we ever want to collect or use children's data in a new way, we ask parents for permission again before it applies; until they agree, nothing changes for their child. For smaller changes, we tell parents and players in the game or by email.
