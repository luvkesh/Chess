Chacklate Chess

Privacy for families

Last updated 25 October 2026. Beta version: the operator details marked below are filled in before public launch.

The short version. Chacklate Chess is made for children. It works fully offline with nothing collected at all. To play online, a parent says yes first. We keep only what the game needs, we never show ads, we use no third-party analytics or tracking, there is no chat, and a parent can see, download or delete everything at any time from the parent page.

Who we are

Chacklate Chess is operated by [company name, registered address] ("we"). Questions, requests and complaints go to [privacy email]. Our grievance officer under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 is [name, email]; we reply within 7 days and resolve within 30.

Playing without an account

Games against the computer, lessons, puzzles and the endgame dojo all run on the device. Progress is saved in the device's own storage and never leaves it. Nothing is sent to us, and no account, name or email is asked for.

Accounts, ages and a parent's permission

When someone makes an account, we ask for their birth month and year (never the day), in a neutral way that does not hint at a "right" answer. That decides which rules apply:

  • Under 18 (under 13 counts as a child under the US COPPA rule; under 18 counts as a child under India's DPDP Act): the player picks a name we generate, such as BraveOtter42, and gives a parent's email. The account stays offline-only until the parent opens the link we send and gives verifiable permission by creating a parent account. If no parent responds within 7 days, the request expires and the parent's email is not used again.
  • 18 and over: a normal account. Names are checked for rude words and contact details.

We do not ask a child for their own email address, phone number, photo, school or location.

What we never do

  • No advertising of any kind, and no behavioural or targeted ads.
  • No third-party analytics, ad SDKs, social logins or tracking pixels.
  • No selling, renting or sharing of personal data.
  • No chat of any kind. Players cannot send each other messages.
  • No public profiles for under-18s, and they are not listed on public leaderboards.
  • No location, contacts, camera or microphone access.
  • No loot boxes, no paid random rewards, no countdown pressure and no messages that guilt a child into playing.

Playing with others

Players under 18 are matched only with other young players. A parent of a teenager can allow games with adults. Friends play together by sharing a short code, which only works between players in the same group. Anyone can report a game, and a person reviews reports.

Purchases

The store sits behind a grown-up check. On an under-18 account, every purchase needs the parent's PIN, and a parent can turn purchases off entirely. Everything a player earns by playing stays theirs, even after a season ends.

Who processes data for us

We use a small number of service providers only to run the game: hosting for the game server, an email service to send the parent permission email, and a payment provider for purchases. They may use the data only to provide that service to us. The providers are named here before launch: [hosting provider], [email provider], [payment provider]. Voices in the game were recorded in advance and play from files; nothing a child says or types is sent anywhere.

A parent's rights

From the parent page, a parent can at any time:

  • see their child's activity and change what the child can do;
  • download all the data we hold about their child;
  • delete the child's account, which removes their games, progress and ratings;
  • withdraw permission, which deletes every linked child account and the parent account.

You can also write to [privacy email] to ask for any of these, to correct something, or to complain. In India you may also complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

Security

Passwords and PINs are stored only as salted hashes. Connections are encrypted. Purchase PINs lock after repeated wrong guesses. Access to the moderation tools is limited to named staff, and every staff action is logged.

Changes

If we change how we handle children's data in a way that matters, we ask parents again before it applies.
